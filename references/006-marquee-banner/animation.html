Marquee banner.

A horizontal scrolling banner that reacts to the direction you scroll the page. Scroll down — it drifts left. Scroll up — it reverses and drifts right. Stop — it resumes its idle leftward drift.

Scroll-aware motion.

The marquee runs continuously at an idle drift to the left. As you scroll, it picks up the rhythm of your reading: scrolling down accelerates the drift in the same direction; scrolling up reverses it.

This subtle responsiveness makes the marquee feel like part of the page rather than an autonomous decoration. The strip becomes a tactile element that reflects your pace and direction.

How it works.

A GSAP timeline drives the marquee's horizontal position on an infinite loop. On every scroll event, the timeScale of that timeline is set to +1 or -1 based on the sign of the scroll delta — the underlying animation does the rest.

A short timeout resets the direction to the default (forward) when scrolling stops, so the marquee always idles in one consistent direction rather than freezing wherever the user happened to stop.

When to use this.

Top-of-page brand ribbons that feel alive without demanding attention. Footers that punctuate a long page. Section dividers between distinct content areas. Hero accents that hint at motion before the user even hovers anything.

Where it loses its charm.

When the words inside are the actual primary message — readers can't read scrolling text comfortably. Use it for atmosphere and rhythm, not for content that needs to be parsed.
